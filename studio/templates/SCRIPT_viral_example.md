# Example: viral + funny fact short (~35 s). Copy this layout to SCRIPT.md for a new story.

Title: The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice.
Format: 9:16, ~3.5 words/s, captions on, deadpan voice, a gag every 4-6 s.

## Facts (with sources, check before recording)
- 1925, Paris: con man Victor Lustig posed as a government official and offered the Eiffel Tower for scrap to metal dealers; André Poisson paid; Lustig fled; Poisson did not report it; Lustig tried again with new buyers and one of them went to the police; Lustig left for America.
- Sources: add 2 reliable links here. Details such as the hotel and the number of dealers vary between sources; keep them generic.

## Voiceover, beat by beat

| Beat | Voice | Picture | Gag / sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook (0-3 s) | "In 1925, one man sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice." | Wide of the tower; a tiny "SOLD" sign slaps on, then "x2" | Slide whistle, cash register ding |
| Setup | "Paris was broke, and the tower cost a fortune to maintain." | Rusty tower, bills piling up on its legs | Deadpan face on a passer-by |
| 2 The pitch (laugh) | "A con man named Victor Lustig noticed. He invited scrap metal dealers to a fancy hotel and posed as a government official." | Hotel room, row of dealers, hero in a too-big official hat | Hat slides over his eyes |
| 3 Looks right | "His pitch? The city secretly wants to sell the tower for scrap. And it's top secret." | Whispering behind a giant TOP SECRET folder | Rule of three: whisper, whisper, shout |
| 4 The clue | "One dealer, André Poisson, wanted the deal badly. Lustig hinted that a bribe would help." | Sweating dealer, cutaway on the envelope | Envelope grows each shot |
| 5 Payoff | "So Poisson paid the bribe... and the full price. Then Lustig left the country with the cash." | Suitcase, train, hero waves | Whoosh, train whistle |
| Twist | "But Poisson was too embarrassed to go to the police. So Lustig came back, and sold it again." | Dealer turns away from the police door; hero returns | Crowd "ooooh" |
| 6 Ending | "This time, the buyer called the cops. And Lustig fled to America." | New buyer runs to police, hero sprints to a ship | Callback: tiny "SOLD" sign falls off the tower |

## Camera plan (continuous flows, almost no cuts)
- **Flow A (0-9 s), one move:** wide pull-back on the tower with the "SOLD x2" signs → tilt down and travel to the broke crowd at its foot (bills piling up on the tower's legs) → settle on one worried face. Rack focus follows each stop.
- **Dissolve (6 f)** into the hotel.
- **Flow B (9-20 s), one move:** low hero angle on the con man in the official hat → orbit to the row of dealers → push to the TOP SECRET folder → rack focus to the sweating dealer's face.
- **Flow C (20-30 s), one move:** envelope macro → follow it into the suitcase → pull back and through the train window to the hero waving; hide the place change in the window.
- **Hard cut (the only one):** to the police door for the gag.
- **Flow D (30-37 s):** dealer turns away → travel back to the hotel → the new buyer runs out; end on the tower with a tiny "SOLD" sign falling off.

## Light plan
Day exterior (tower) · warm interior with window light (hotel) · cool dusk with a warm station lamp (train) · day exterior (finale).

## Last line (comment bait)
End on the callback, then a debatable question in the description ("Would you have bought it?").

## Optional dialogue beat (narrator reads it, faces sell it)
Between beats 4 and 5, the narrator says: "Is this legal?" the dealer asked. "Very," said Lustig.
- Flow stop on the dealer (sweating, `nervous`), 20 f creep while the line is read.
- 8 f of silence with a slow push-in.
- Rack focus to Lustig, who answers with a `smile` 4 f after "Very".
- Hold the dealer's relieved `relief` face for 10 f with a tiny "ding" SFX. No cut; the camera orbits between them inside the same flow.
